01 / THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Collaboration has a cost.
When does it pay off?

Multi-agent systems expand what models can search, verify, and solve. But repeated inference, growing message histories, and coordination can outweigh those gains.

This survey connects the benefit of collaboration to its end-to-end resource cost. It first asks when collaboration is worth invoking, then organizes efficiency methods by the object they control.

05 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Open questions.
Practical directions.

01 Comparable evaluation +

Can shared budgets, matched baselines, and quality–resource curves make efficiency claims comparable across tasks and model pools?

02 Adaptive control +

Can a controller predict when another collaborative action is worth its incremental cost, then continue, escalate, or stop as evidence arrives?

03 Transfer under drift +

How long does an optimized configuration retain its advantage as models, tools, prices, and workloads change?

04 Causal attribution +

Does the gain come from the graph, role prompts, model access, information routing, or simply additional samples? Matched trace interventions can separate these effects.

05 End-to-end cost +

Can logical work, state reuse, and scheduling be optimized jointly while including tool execution, recovery, defenses, and persistent memory?
